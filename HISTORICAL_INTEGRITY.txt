THE SPICE RACE — HARD DESIGN RULE: HISTORICAL INTEGRITY

Gameplay must never override historical accuracy.

The simulation may simplify history to make complex systems understandable to students, but it must not invent, distort, or knowingly perpetuate misleading historical narratives merely to improve gameplay.

1. Established historical events and chronology remain anchored to the historical record.
2. Students control their assigned European power, not world history.
3. Non-European societies remain independent historical actors with their own political, economic, cultural, and strategic interests.
4. Counterfactual student choices must be historically plausible and clearly labeled as counterfactual.
5. The interface must distinguish WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED from WHAT THE STUDENT CHOSE.
6. Anachronistic modern nation-state labels should be avoided when they distort historical political reality.
7. Religion is treated as a genuine historical force interacting with economic, political, dynastic, and cultural motives.
8. Existing Afro-Eurasian and Indigenous systems must not be depicted as empty spaces awaiting Europeans.
9. Educational simplifications must be disclosed through Historical Record / What We Simplified notes.
10. Major historical claims should have a reference trail suitable for later source verification.

Historical complexity is a feature, not a problem to be designed away.
