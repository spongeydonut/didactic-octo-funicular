Core causal lesson

European political stabilization helped revive commerce and luxury consumption. Asian goods were highly desired. Long-distance trade added transport, intermediary, political, and risk costs. Profit and state competition encouraged investment in maritime alternatives. Those searches intersected with existing Afro-Eurasian systems and eventually with colonization and imperial competition.

Recurring lenses

Economics: supply, demand, scarcity, transport, intermediaries, tolls, investment, profit.
Politics: stability, diplomacy, taxation, competition, state capacity, sovereignty.
Power: religion, naval force, ports, territory, labor, resources, human consequences.

Exit ticket

  1. Why could Asian luxury goods become expensive in European markets?
  2. How can political stability or instability affect trade?
  3. Why did competition among European states encourage maritime exploration?
  4. Explain the causal chain from European demand for Asian goods to colonial expansion.

Continuity

The interface is designed to continue into The Atlantic World, Colonial America, Revolution, and western expansion without resetting the underlying systems of trade, land, resources, labor, sovereignty, diplomacy, and human cost.
